# Scope and limitations

The results support a bounded comparison of synthetic resting EEG and eye-state classification in the completed MPI-LEMON experiment.

1. **Known-participant evaluation.** The same 198 participants occur in all splits. Complete blocks are held out; unseen-person, new-session, new-device, and cross-dataset generalization were not established.
2. **Development-informed selection.** Generator choices and the final utility training policy used Validation feedback. Both B2 cohorts labeled VAL_DEV and VAL_CONF were development evidence. The supplementary Validation-reference analysis is exploratory and post hoc: Validation was held out from generator gradient training, but spectral fidelity informed SSC-C selection with different aggregation and additional eligibility criteria. Its SSC-C-versus-SSC comparison remains selection-dependent, not independent confirmation. Freezing supplementary settings and subsets before waveform access does not remove earlier selection dependence or make the study preregistered.
3. **Unequal model histories.** B0, B1, and B2 have different training and selection histories. Their comparison does not isolate the causal effect of adding participant identity. The selected B2 checkpoint does not demonstrate reproducibility of its continuation recipe across training seeds.
4. **Reference and sampling dependence.** Original fidelity and fingerprints are relative to Real Train, which also informed SC/SSC selection. The separate Real–Real calibration uses Train data available during fitting and does not establish a reliability ceiling or equivalence. The supplementary Validation reference uses different blocks of the same participants. Its single count-matched subset equalizes 15–25 windows per participant/state, not their temporal dependence or block diversity. Neither analysis is an independent generative-fidelity Test evaluation. Identification remains below real block-to-block retrieval; continuation's EO ordering varies with the reference, query count, and top-1 versus top-5 metric.
5. **Classifier-dependent utility.** Full-dose B2 augmentation improves this EEGNet comparison but reduces both classical classifiers' accuracy. Its advantage over B1 is not decisive. Half-dose and B0 results prevent a simple claim that more synthetic data always helps.
6. **Training exposure differs.** A full epoch contains 374, 560, or 748 optimizer steps depending on the training condition. Equal epoch ceilings do not equalize update budgets. The Real duplicate control adds exposure without adding signal diversity; it matches nominal full-augmentation size, not selected-checkpoint optimizer updates. The final manuscript's post hoc SSC-C-versus-duplication interval includes zero, so superiority to that control is unestablished. Noise injection, temporal shifts, and reduced-data fitting of both generator and classifier remain untested.
7. **Limited uncertainty scope.** EEGNet population SD summarizes three classifier-training seeds sharing fixed synthetic pools. Utility subject-bootstrap CIs summarize participant variability, with matched seeds averaged within participant. Supplementary fidelity intervals use 5,000 paired participant resamples with seed 2026101703, conditional on the fixed data, models, pools, and one selected subset. Neither analysis resamples generator training or synthetic pools; intervals are unadjusted for multiple comparisons and do not capture all training or selection uncertainty. The fixed 8–13 Hz alpha band does not adapt to individual alpha peak frequency.
8. **TSTR includes real-data selection.** TSTR classifier gradient updates use synthetic signals, while EEGNet checkpoint selection uses real Validation. It is not a wholly synthetic end-to-end selection pipeline.
9. **Privacy is unestablished.** No exact-copy detections within the specified strata do not rule out memorization, membership inference, or reidentification. Fingerprint recognition is a conditional-fidelity descriptor, not a privacy guarantee. The supplementary nearest-Train RMSE reference places SSC-C modestly closer to Train than real Validation, but cannot separate distributional similarity, between-block variation, and approximate memorization. There is no calibrated copying threshold or equivalence margin.
10. **Connectivity is sensor-space.** Debiased squared wPLI describes channel-level phase relationships, not anatomical or causal connectivity. Finite negative estimates are retained; high ordinary channel-correlation similarity does not establish strong phase-connectivity preservation.
11. **Demographic analyses are limited.** The local distributed participant table used by the project has missing demographic values. The study does not establish demographic representativeness or subgroup fairness.
12. **Public reproduction has boundaries.** The release preserves code and evidence but excludes raw signals, processed datasets, model checkpoints, and full synthetic pools. The new Validation subset also excludes participant rows, caches, the original frozen plan, window indices, and waveform illustrations. Offline checks can validate hashes, schemas, arithmetic, and reported aggregate values; they cannot independently reproduce signal features, paired bootstraps, or original participant-level checks. Historical scripts retain platform, path, artifact, and hash assumptions. The separately named portable Validation derivative has explicit configuration and hash-preserving path resolution, but full external-data execution remains untested in this update. No full-pipeline replication is claimed.

Future work should distinguish a new research protocol from reproduction of the frozen study. Existing Final Test outcomes must not become a development set for further tuning.
